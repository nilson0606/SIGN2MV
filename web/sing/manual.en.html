NEW · SEGMENT RECORDING AND RETAKES

Sing it segment by segment, then assemble a full take

  1. Load the song first, then press Enable recording mode below the player. Sing from the start and score and whole-song scoring stay as they were; if a performance has not been scored yet, score it before switching.
  2. Play the video and press Split here where a new segment begins; playback is not interrupted. You do not need to decide the number of segments in advance.
  3. Pressed it late? Drag the boundary on the timeline, or open Edit boundaries / Segment names to type mm:ss.sss, move it 0.1 or 1 second earlier or later, and preview the 3 seconds before and after it. Boundaries can be undone or removed. On the timeline the selected segment (the one to record) is bright and the others are dimmed; the segment being recorded flashes.
  4. Select a segment and press Loop this segment to practise; when you are ready press Record this segment. By default playback starts 5 seconds early; in “Start and tail” you can enter a whole number of seconds from 0 to 60. If there is not enough time before the segment, playback starts from the beginning of the song. The tail margin is 2 seconds by default. “Guide vocals while recording” has the same options as whole-song singing: original vocals + backing by default, the extra “Original vocals + backing (no harmonies)” for lead / harmony versions, or backing / harmonies only. The two non-default items mute the video and play the saved separated stems instead; wear headphones so the original vocals are not picked up by the microphone.
  5. Every take is saved on its own. Once it is saved the microphone turns off, and the next time you press Record this segment it turns on again. While a take is being saved (“Saving this part's take…” is shown) the next segment cannot be recorded. Open Choose a take for this segment to choose the segment's take. Pick the best take of each segment, then assemble the full performance.
  6. Open Assemble a full mix / Save when satisfied and in “Mix content” choose vocals + backing / harmonies (default) or vocals only. Versions separated into lead / harmonies also have “Vocals + backing (no harmonies)”: neither the assembled mix nor the saved result has the harmony stem, and later post-production does not add it back. The vocal correction follows the “Input latency” by default (factory setting 400 ms); once you change it here, the number here is remembered and no longer follows. Recordings keep the raw vocals and the correction is applied only when previewing and assembling, so you can adjust it before or after recording. After listening with “Assemble full mix” or “Assemble and preview this join”, press “Download mix WAV”, or press Save for post-production to keep the full result for vocal production and A/B.

The Vocal / backing balance on the right shares its settings with “Sing from the start and score” and is remembered. It balances automatically by default; tick manual to set the vocal and backing levels yourself, with small automatic corrections still applied. Single-segment previews and full mixes use the current setting; after changing it, preview or assemble again. The raw vocals and saved results are not rewritten, and the guide-vocal volume while recording is not affected.

Recording mode does not write a whole-song score. If a segment is missing, or a moved boundary falls outside the recorded range, you are asked to record it; silence is left only when “Allow silence in unrecorded regions” is ticked. Every raw take is kept, and each assembled mix is saved as a new result.

Boundaries and take choices are kept as a draft in the SIGN2MV window's browser data. Every change is saved automatically, with nothing to press; load the same song again to carry on. When you come back to the Sing & Record page from another page, the takes of each segment are read again; the audio stays in the song library. Do not delete a raw take the draft is using; if it was deleted, assembling tells you the source is missing.

The top of the main screen lists the entry points for audio input, song, playback / recording, song preparation, scoring and post-production, with no menus to open. The background colour follows scrolling to mark the current block; a dashed frame and a “Next” hint guide you through the flow: check audio input → choose a song → song preparation and key → singing settings → sing and record → preview → save and post-process when satisfied. Completed items are skipped. Settings can carry over from last time; after checking them press “Settings confirmed”. On a narrow screen, swipe the entry points sideways. Switching blocks only moves the view; it does not stop singing or recording.

EVERY TIME

Start with these 6 steps

  1. Import the song in the SIGN2MV Library (YouTube or a local file) and press Sing this song; or choose it under Song (SIGN2MV library) at the top of the Sing & Record page and press Load song.
  2. The first time you sing a song (or a key), press Prepare song reference and wait until it says it is ready. Keys that already have a reference are listed in the song menu and load as soon as you choose them.
  3. Wear headphones, enable the microphone, and check that the level meter moves when you speak.
  4. In Scoring settings choose the scoring difficulty and scoring range, then press Sing from the start and score. Once it has started, the page scrolls to the player and the video restarts from 0 seconds; separated-stem previews, recordings and post-production previews stop so nothing overlaps.
  5. When you have finished, press Finish and score. To sing the same song again, just press Sing from the start and score.
  6. To recalculate the take you just sang with another latency or difficulty, use Rescore next to Finish and score (see “Latency calibration and rescoring”); there is no need to sing again.

Theme at the top of the Sing & Record page offers “Console”, “Tech blue” or “Warm”. Console is a dark grey-blue hardware panel with recessed input boxes, raised keys, fader-style sliders and block numbers in LED digit windows; Tech blue uses a midnight-blue background with a cyan glow; Warm uses a dark coffee background with amber accents. The default is Console; your choice is remembered and applies at once. A fixed section bar at the top of the main screen jumps to each block; Help ↗ opens this page.

“Scoring range” defaults to Performed range only, so the parts you have not sung yet cost no points. To take on the whole song, switch it to “Whole song” yourself.

STEP 01 · ONLY A NEW SONG NEEDS A WAIT

Build the song reference

Song key: below the player, choose the original key or −12 to +12 semitones, then prepare the song; if the song is already loaded, press “Create / Load this key”. The stems and scoring reference of each key are saved together in the song library, grouped under the same song; recordings, segment drafts and the takes chosen for each segment are kept separately. The first time you open a key that has not been split yet, it starts from the boundaries and part names of the key of the same song changed most recently (only for the same separation version and the same length; takes are not carried, so record them again in this key), and the status line says which key they came from; if you do not want them, press “Undo split” to go back to one part. A key that already has boundaries is left as it is. A key change reuses the original-key separated stems without separating again; tempo and timeline stay the same. When the key is not 0 the video's own sound is muted automatically and the local key-shifted stems play in sync; the guide-vocal options are available as usual (lead / harmony versions also have “Original vocals + backing (no harmonies)”). Returning to the original key restores the player's previous mute state. After choosing another key, press Create / Load for it to take effect; existing recordings are not shifted automatically.

“Separation and pitch settings · Masks and previews” is folded by default. Open it to see the default settings, preview the separation and edit unscored masks; “Advanced separation settings” is folded too, and lets you choose the separation model, pipeline, lead / harmonies, pitch method and analysis range.

  1. Choose a song under Song (SIGN2MV library) at the top and press Load song. A new song must first be imported in the SIGN2MV library; a version with clear original vocals gives more accurate separation and scoring.
  2. New songs use the whole song (up to 15 minutes), RMVPE, Demucs / htdemucs, single-pass separation and vocals / instrumental separation by default, and keep the separated stems.
  3. Just press Prepare song reference, outside the block; there is no need to open the settings.
  4. Wait for Ready before previewing or singing. After separation reaches 100% the stems, the reference and saving still have to finish; progress, errors and the cancel button stay outside the folded block.

Existing versions in the song library still load with their original settings; they are not converted to the new settings. To redo such a version use “Separate again and overwrite”; if its stems are missing use “Rebuild preview stems”; both keep the existing version's settings. A normal “Prepare song reference” uses the current options, and folding the block does not reset them. Changing the model or mode saves a different version; “Reset separation defaults” returns to the original defaults.

The models are downloaded at installation; the GPU is used when available, otherwise the CPU. Audio is processed and saved only on this computer.

STEP 02 · CHECK BY EAR FIRST

Preview the separation

  1. Load a song from the song library, or wait for a new song to finish preparing.
  2. Open Separation and pitch settings · Masks and previews and check the song, model and pipeline shown after “Loaded:” under Preview separated stems.
  3. Press Preview vocals or Preview instrumental; the player below can pause and seek. A preview pauses the video and is not scored.
  4. If an old version has only the reference, press Rebuild preview stems. Previews are available once it is ready.

Existing BS-RoFormer, Mel-Band RoFormer, inverse-subtraction, YIN and clip versions can still be loaded from the song library; lead versions that already have four stems keep the lead / harmony previews. New songs can also use these models and pipelines in the advanced separation settings. To keep the original harmonies when singing with “Backing / harmonies only”, choose “Lead / Harmony” and keep the stems when preparing the song; old songs with only two stems need this new version made, while existing four-stem versions load as they are.

Folding this block only changes what is shown; it does not stop a playing preview, the video, the microphone or a recording. To stop a preview, use the pause button of the audio player.

RMVPE and existing reference versions

New songs use RMVPE to extract the vocal pitch by default; the model is downloaded at installation and shared by all songs. The pitch model does not change the sound of the preview stems or the way the microphone is analysed live.

Old YIN versions are kept. After choosing a version in the song library, check the model actually used in the labels of the song library and the preview source. Saved versions with other models, clips and masks are not rewritten by this rework of the page.

OPTIONAL · NO MASKS BY DEFAULT

Instruments taken for singing? Set unscored regions

  1. Load an already separated song from the local playlist; there is no need to separate it again.
  2. Open Separation and pitch settings · Masks and previews and find Unscored region masks below. It is best to set them before enabling the microphone; if a round is in progress, finish and score it before editing.
  3. Play to the part you want to exclude and press Use current position as start; at its end press Use current position as end. You can also type seconds or min:sec, such as 32.5 or 1:02.5.
  4. Press Add and save mask and check that it says it was saved. You can add several regions; overlapping or touching regions are merged. If saving fails, the new mask is not applied; read the error and try again.
  5. Once you start singing, the original notes on the right show Rest inside masked regions and the blue reference line breaks; pitch, rhythm and completeness are not counted there. The video plays normally and is not muted.
  6. To change a region, delete it and add it again; Clear all masks returns to scoring with the original reference.

Masks are saved automatically in the local song library you chose, tied to the original video and the analysis range. The Demucs / BS-RoFormer / Mel-Band RoFormer, vocals / lead and YIN / RMVPE versions of the same video and analysis range share one mask file: a change made in any version also applies to the others the next time they load, so changing the model or redoing a version needs no new marks. Masks apply only to scoring and the reference display; the audio is not cut. Of older existing masks, the most recently saved non-empty one is used; later changes go to the shared file.

The start is included and the end is not. Regions are excluded in reference time steps (about 0.1 s at present), and every step that overlaps a region is excluded whole. Masks are fixed once a round starts, even while it is paused, and can be changed again after scoring. They apply to both scoring ranges (whole song / performed range); a round that passes only rests or masked regions produces no score.

First choose the input mode and microphone in Audio input near the top of the page. On a desktop, the first row has the song and player on the left and live pitch and audio input on the right; below them come Separation and pitch extraction, the song reference list, Recording post-production, 04 Latency calibration, and Scoring and recording settings. Enable / stop microphone is in the live pitch block.

Scoring difficulty, rescoring difficulty and reference, octave matching and scoring range are in Scoring settings, folded by default; open it to change them. Recording content, vocal timing and the volume balance are in “Recording settings”. “Sing from the start and score”, “Finish and score”, the recording status and the score are below the video and stay visible.

STEP 03 · SET BEFORE EACH ROUND

Enable the microphone and sing from the start

  1. Put on headphones, choose the Microphone, press Enable microphone and allow the permission. Speak and check that the level meter and the pitch react.
  2. In Scoring difficulty above “Sing from the start and score”, choose standard, strict or relaxed. The default is Standard; relaxed suits casual practice and strict suits precise practice. It is fixed once a round starts, and the round's difficulty is shown above the score.
  3. In Octave matching, keep Allow octave differences, so that a male voice can sing a female melody an octave lower; choose exact pitch only to require the same pitch as the original singer.
  4. Choose the Scoring range. The default, Performed range only, scores only the playback between start and stop; missed or skipped singing inside it still loses points. Only Whole song counts the rest of the song as missed when you stop early.
  5. Press Sing from the start and score. The microphone is enabled, the player returns to 0 seconds, the previous round's data is cleared and scoring begins. Playback restarts even if it was already playing. Once the microphone is ready and the player is confirmed at 0 seconds, the page scrolls to the player, clear of the fixed bar at the top, so you do not have to look for the video.
  6. Watch the live pitch graph while you sing: the bright line is your voice (cyan in the cool themes, amber in the warm theme) and the blue line is the original reference; with octave differences allowed, the two lines need not overlap.

Once the song reference and the microphone are ready, you can also just press play on the player; scoring starts from the current position. For consistent practice from the beginning every time, use Sing from the start and score.

How do the three difficulties compare?

DifficultyFull pitch scoreZero pitch scoreEarly / late without pitch lossFull onset scoreZero onset score
Relaxed±50 cents150 cents±0.2 s±0.2 s0.5 s
Standard (default)±30 cents105 cents±0.1 s±0.1 s0.3 s
Strict±20 cents55 centsnone±0.05 s0.2 s

100 cents = 1 semitone. Standard: a little more than a semitone off (singing the neighbouring note) earns no pitch points; relaxed: anything within a quarter tone counts as right; strict: a little more than a quarter tone off earns no points. The pitch thresholds of standard and strict each keep 5 extra cents for measurement error: running “Score the original vocal” on the original-key original vocals of the song 童話, the original singer reaches 100 for pitch only with those 5 cents. “Early / late without pitch loss” means that when you sing a little early or late (diction, runs), the pitch is still compared. The song reference has one step every 0.1 s, so onset timing is also accurate to 0.1 s. Rhythm is measured at every onset of the song reference: around the onset, the time the voice “arrives at this note” is found (closer to this note than to the previous one and within 150 cents; after silence, the time the voice starts), in the same way for the song reference and for your voice, and the two times are compared. So when the timing is right, a slightly wrong pitch does not give the whole onset 0 points; pitch is penalised under pitch. Missed singing still loses points; completeness and the weights of the three parts are calculated as before.

Difficulty, allowing octave differences and the whole-song / performed scoring range are three separate settings. Changing difficulty does not need the song separated again; after scoring, choose the next round's difficulty and press “Sing from the start and score”. When a song is first ready you can also press play on the player, which uses the difficulty chosen at that moment; to sing again after scoring, press “Sing from the start and score”.

These are practice thresholds defined by this tool and not yet calibrated against human judges. “Standard” is not an industry-wide standard; separation quality and input latency also affect the score. The score history keeps only the song title and the score, not the difficulty, so history scores from different difficulties should not be compared directly.

The score is pitch 60%, rhythm 25% and completeness 15%. Lyrics are not recognised: a wrong word sung on the right pitch cannot be detected. The settings are fixed once a round starts and can be changed for the next round.

Performance recordings and score history

  1. Open Recording settings and find Save performance recordings. The recording content can be “Singer” or “Singer + backing / harmonies” (default); “Do not save recordings” turns recording off. This browser remembers the choice; with recording off, the microphone is used only for scoring and the recorder does not start.
  2. Below the player, before the “Sing from the start and score” button, open Vocal / backing balance. “Manual levels + automatic fine-tuning” is unticked by default, so the singer and the backing / harmonies are balanced automatically, each corrected by up to ±6 dB. Tick it to set the singer and backing / harmony volumes yourself (0–200%); the system takes them as the base and fine-tunes only ±3 dB; 0% stays silent. The two percentages are separate volumes and need not add up to 100%. The setting is remembered and fixed once a round starts; singer mode does not use the backing / harmony slider; backing and harmonies are adjusted together as one, keeping their relative balance.
  3. Mixing needs saved separated stems; if there are none, rebuild them first. The loaded version decides what is added: an ordinary two-stem version adds the instrumental; a lead / harmony four-stem version adds the instrumental + harmonies. Both stems follow play, pause and seek in sync; if an expected stem is missing you are asked to rebuild it, and harmonies are never silently left out. The original lead vocal or full vocals are never added, and the sound of the video playback is not captured. The recordings list shows whether there is a separate harmony stem. Wear headphones so the speakers do not leak into the microphone again.
  4. Press Sing from the start and score; once the microphone and player are in sync, recording starts as the video plays. Recording pauses when the video pauses or buffers; seeking follows the new position, and the recording keeps the order you actually sang. Singer mode mixes in no instrumental or harmonies at all.
  5. Pressing Stop input or Finish and score, or the video ending, stops the recording automatically and keeps it under “Whole-song recordings · Pending review” below the player. Preview it as vocals + backing (default) or vocals only; when you are happy press “Save for post-production” to add it to the post-production menu, or delete the take if not. Pending recordings survive a page reload, and “Download this take” saves a copy. If you reload, close the window or capture is interrupted while singing, what was already recorded is completed and moved the next time the page opens, marked “Recovered after an interruption”, and also placed in pending review. Leaving the page while a round is being scored or a recording saved asks you first. Pressing “Sing from the start and score” again saves the previous part first and starts a new one without overwriting it; pressing play on the player or testing the microphone alone does not start a new recording.
  6. In Recording post-production, open Whole-song takes and saved results to preview, download or delete individual recordings. New recordings are saved as lossless WAV. Finished recordings are kept in the song library's recordings folder; until saving has finished they stay in the browser, so check the recording location message.
  7. Open Score history · Select to clear, tick the entries to delete and press Clear selected scores; you can select all, or just press Clear everything. These delete only scores; recordings and the song library are not affected.

The instrumental covers only the analysis range used when the song was prepared; beyond the stem length the recording still keeps the vocals. Automatic recording volume adjusts smoothly; below the threshold, or without steady singing, the microphone is not boosted; the output has compression and peak protection. This is volume balancing, not automatic EQ, and it cannot repair a signal the microphone has already clipped. The mix does not use the microphone signal used for pitch scoring, and masks affect only scoring; they never mute the recording. Bluetooth and device latency can still make it sound out of sync.

Moving to the background or changing devices stops capture and tries to save. During recording, audio chunks are saved to a local database about every second; after an unexpected close only the chunks already written are kept, and the list marks the recording as not ended normally. If storage runs out, “Download unsaved recording” appears; download a backup before closing the page.

STEP 05 · NO SEPARATING AGAIN NEXT TIME

Load, redo and delete

  1. Press Song library at the top of the main screen: the page scrolls to the separate local playlist block and opens the list; you can also click the large bright title Local playlist · Click a song to load to open or fold it. The number of saved references is shown below; press Refresh library if needed.
  2. Click a song name to load it; the previous song's in-memory work is unloaded, but its saved data is not deleted.
  3. To redo: load the version to redo from the song library, then press Separate again and overwrite. The cache is skipped, and the result for the same video, range, model, pipeline, pitch method and mode is replaced only once the new one succeeds.
  4. To remove: press Delete on the entry; that reference and its preview audio are deleted. Versions with other models, ranges or modes are not affected.

After a song's original is replaced with “Import again (replace source)” in the SIGN2MV library, its old references are marked “Source replaced: made again when loaded” and are rebuilt automatically when loaded; the pitch and instrumental of the old original are not reused.

If a new separation fails, its new output is cleared; the saved old result is kept until a new result succeeds. Different models, analysis ranges and separation modes are separate entries. To switch to an existing version, load its entry from the song library.

Finish and score a performance in progress before redoing, rebuilding or changing songs. Cancel / Unload in the song block releases the current work; it does not delete anything from the song library.

STEP 04 · OPTIONAL

Latency calibration and rescoring

04 Latency calibration: wear headphones and press Start calibration (if the microphone is off it is enabled automatically, using the microphone chosen in 03). After a countdown of 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, sing “Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star” following the prompts (about 30 seconds; solfège, “la” or the words are all fine, and men can sing an octave lower). The first line is a warm-up, marked “Warm-up · not scored”; it is neither scored nor used by the automatic search. Afterwards it is analysed with RMVPE and only the Rhythm score counts. It first runs Find it automatically (it scans 100–500 ms, computing the rhythm score every 10 ms, takes the middle of the highest-scoring stretch and draws the rhythm curve; if the best score touches 100 or 500, it warns you that the real value may lie outside the range). You can also change the latency and press Rescore (no need to sing again) to compare; when you are satisfied, press Apply and finish to save it as the input latency. Playback, capture, recording and scoring take the same path as real singing. The reference it is compared with is placed where a real singer's voice falls: when a person starts a note, the moment its pitch can be heard is naturally a little after the beat (measured on studio original vocals, about 40 ms later than a synthesised prompt tone), so the reference for scoring and the automatic search sits 41 ms after the beat, while the prompt tones you hear stay exactly on the beat. The latency found this way matches the latency that scores best when you sing real songs. “Latency calibration” in the top bar and this block are marked in green, because the input latency affects how all scoring and recording are aligned. The step guide at the top of the page asks you to calibrate or skip the first time; you can come back at any time. The old “Watch the beat, adjust timing” block (beat light, five-note test) has been removed from the page.

The input latency defaults to +400 ms, moving the singer's voice 0.40 seconds earlier: singing at 10.400 s is compared with the song reference at 10.000 s. Live scoring and the vocal correction of whole-song recordings share this number; it is applied once and never added up. A change is remembered and is not reset when you change the input device. After singing, changing the number in “Scoring delay” or pressing “Rescore” makes the following rounds of this song use that number; changing songs or reloading returns to the setting here. The original vocal test does not change it.

The “Take to rescore” menu chooses which take to rescore: the one just sung, or a saved whole-song recording of this song and key (both pending recordings and those saved for post-production are listed, newest first; choosing one brings in the latency used when it was recorded). So after reloading or reopening there is no need to sing again: choose the last recording to recalculate with another latency or difficulty. The first rescore of a saved recording first analyses its clean vocals with RMVPE for a few seconds and saves the result in that recording, so later scores are immediate; the difficulty is the one currently chosen above “Sing from the start and score”. Rescoring is not possible while singing.

“AI cover” below the menu lists the AI cover versions of this song and key (the cover's key must match the loaded reference). Scoring uses the converted vocals of the cover; they are aligned with the song, so the scoring delay starts at 0, and changing it does not touch the input latency. A pitch shift set on the version itself (such as −12 for a male / female conversion) is added back for scoring, so the score shows how well the converted melody is kept. The first scoring after each page opening analyses for a few seconds with RMVPE, without writing to the cover files; for versions converted separately for male and female parts only the main pitch shift is added back, so the score may be inaccurate.

🧪 Score the original vocal (next to “Finish and score”) uses the separated original vocals instead of the microphone and runs exactly the same scoring and recording flow as your singing, to show what score this song's reference and scoring give the original singer; when a score is low, it helps tell a reference problem from a singing problem. The original vocals lack the delay of “played from the speaker, heard, then sung”, so they are automatically brought in later by the input latency and settled the same way as your singing. This recording is labelled “original vocal test” and does not change the input latency.

Recording post-production: fix the timing without singing again

Select a saved result and press “Quick MV” or “Full MV”: the recording (with its saved levels, effects and trim) is saved as this song's MV audio, and SIGN2MV switches to the matching MV page. Finished MVs are played, downloaded and deleted on the MV page. If you have just changed settings, render again and select the new result first.

“Preview / Vocals only / Quick MV / Full MV / Download / Post-production / Delete” below the recordings menu act only on the selected recording; Post-production moves to the timing and tone settings, and Delete asks first. “Clear all” at the end of the same row clears the whole post-production list (see “Delete” below).

Song reference, rescoring difficulty, timing rescoring and scoring diagnostics are in “Rescore recording” under “Scoring settings”. Rendering, vocal smoothing and MP3 download are in “Recording post-production”. The recording choice and timing field of the two blocks stay in sync; difficulty defaults to standard.

Preview the full result or the vocals only

After selecting a recording, press “Preview” to play the full result, or “Vocals only” to hear the singer alone, without backing or harmonies. The vocal preview uses this recording's saved volume, timing and smoothing; after changing settings, render again before you can hear the new effect. The vocal preview does not save a recording and does not need the song library's backing. The button is disabled for old mixes that did not keep the raw vocals.

Adjust the lead and backing volumes

Select a recording that can be post-processed, or a new-format result, and adjust “Singer volume” and “Backing / harmony volume”. 100% keeps the balance from recording, 0% is silent, and the maximum is 200%; the two percentages need not add up to 100%. Backing and harmonies share one volume; a vocals-only recording has no backing to adjust. Press “Render again (levels / timing / tone / trim)” to save a copy and preview it; moving the sliders does not change a result that is already playing. Every version keeps its own raw vocals and current settings, so you can keep post-processing, turn smoothing off or readjust the volume later. Raised volumes still pass through peak protection.

Vocal cleanup and pitch correction

All four are off by default and process only the saved raw vocals; A and B are adjusted separately and the difference table shows them, and a saved copy keeps the settings and the raw vocals. Noise reduction estimates the noise floor from quiet, unpitched gaps and applies spectral attenuation, skipping when it finds no gaps; sibilance uses dynamic high-frequency attenuation with adjustable strength and a 3–10 kHz detection frequency; breaths are identified by having no pitch, their band energy, their short length and their nearness to singing — it is not a word-recognising AI and may take breathy notes or consonants for breaths, which region volume can make up for. Pitch correction offers the nearest semitone or the original-singer reference at recording time; the latter finds its targets from the saved playback segments and vocal correction, keeps the octave you sang, and does not correct rests, unscored regions or notes more than two semitones away. Strength 0–100%, correction speed 20–500 ms and preserve vibrato can be set separately. The speed is not a playback delay.

Open “Advanced vocal production” → “Vocal cleanup and pitch correction”. Try noise reduction 25–40%, sibilance 30%, breath reduction 30% and pitch 30–50% first; compare on the same clip before saving a copy. More strength does not mean more natural, and each of the four can be turned off. Processing runs in the background in the local browser; pitch correction keeps the original audio's sample count and timing, and the backing does not change; rescoring still uses the raw vocals.

Advanced vocal production

Space presets: a preset brings the reverb and delay settings into the current A / B set in one step, leaving the other set as it is; after manual tweaks it shows “Custom space effects”. The default is “No reverb or delay” (both 0%), which also turns the space effects off in one click; Hall · Pop is reverb 20% + delay 10%; the delay of Dreamy · Ethereal delay is 12% / 600 ms / 3 repeats / 30% kept each time / alternating left and right, and its interval can be matched to the song's tempo. EQ, compression, smoothing, volume, correction and trim are kept. Region values are kept; the space character and delay interval still follow the global settings. After choosing a preset press “Preview A” / “Preview B” to hear it, and render again to save a copy when satisfied. Changing only the “Space type” still updates just the suggested decay and pre-delay.

In “Advanced vocal production”, both A and B start with reverb 0% and delay 0% (no space effects); the space type stays at Hall with a 1.8-second decay and 25 ms pre-delay, and the delay at 300 ms / 3 repeats / 40% kept each time / centre, so raising the reverb or delay level gives the hall sound. For a recording already saved with production settings, both sets use the saved values. Region volume: press “Add region” and fill in start, end and vocal volume, in seconds of the recording player; 100% is unchanged, 0% silent, up to 200%; regions may not overlap. The seconds are not song time; after changing the timing, check the regions again. Vocal fade out / in: press “Add fade out / in” and fill in start, end and type. “Fade out to 0” lowers the vocals to 0 within the region and keeps them at 0 until the next “Fade in from 0”; “Fade in from 0” raises the vocals back to their normal volume within the region. Any number of regions, no overlaps, and fade-outs and fade-ins must alternate (two fade-outs in a row give a warning); if the first one is a fade-in, the vocals are at 0 when the song starts. The fade follows a curve that sounds even; backing and harmonies are not affected, and the seconds work as for region volume. EQ: low controls body, mid controls presence, high controls brightness; start by comparing ±2–3 dB. Compression: light keeps more dynamics, medium is steadier. Reverb: 0% is off, up to 100%; decay 0.2–10 seconds, pre-delay 0–500 ms. The Legacy defaults are 0.8 s / 15 ms.

These effects apply only to the singer, not to backing / harmonies. After saving a copy with “Render again”, compare with the full preview or “Vocals only”. Each result keeps its own settings and can be edited further when selected; every render starts from the raw vocals, so effects and tails never accumulate. “Reset all vocal effects” undoes them; then render again. Outside a volume region you may still hear reverb carried over naturally from before; a singer volume of 0% overall mutes the reverb too.

Vocal smoothing: compare from light first

  1. Choose a recording with saved raw vocals and check that it can be rendered again.
  2. Set the timing; to compare only the tone, keep the same correction as the original result.
  3. “Vocal smoothing” is off by default; choose Light (slightly softer), Medium (noticeably warmer) or Strong (thick, soft, darker). You can compare off and strong directly, then choose the amount you like.
  4. Press “Render again (levels / timing / tone / trim)”; a new entry is saved, the list and the download file name show the smoothing strength, and the original recording is kept.
  5. Preview with the player below; when satisfied you can convert to MP3. Results can be post-processed further; to compare other strengths, render again with the same volume and timing.

The second version of smoothing combines high-frequency reduction, an upper-mid dip and a high-frequency cut so the three strengths differ more clearly, then uses dynamic EQ to tame harsh moments further. It processes only the singer track and does not change backing, harmonies, the pitch reference or the scores. It is not breath removal and cannot guarantee removing breathiness or repairing cracked notes; too much strength can affect tone and diction. Rescoring still uses the raw vocals. A new-format result saves its own raw vocals and production data, so it can be edited again directly. Old results without raw vocals still have to be handled from the source recording.

The recorded vocal timing correction of new recordings follows the input latency by default (factory setting +400 ms): after stopping, the vocals in the result are moved 0.40 seconds earlier automatically while backing and harmonies stay in place, and the corrected result is saved as WAV. The recordings list and the download file name show the correction actually applied, with the default suffix +400ms (for example Title+400ms.wav or Title+400ms.mp3); rescoring alone does not change the suffix; the clean raw vocals keep their original format and timing. The value is the input latency in “Latency calibration”, adjustable before each round; 0 means no correction; live scoring uses the same number. Post-production pre-fills the round's recording correction and always recalculates from the uncorrected raw vocals, never adding it twice. Old recordings and existing corrected results are not rewritten automatically; if automatic correction fails, the uncorrected recording is kept and the reason is shown.

Delete: delete recordings one by one in the list, or press “Clear all” and confirm. The matching audio, clean vocals, rescoring data and converted MP3 are deleted; “Clear all” clears every entry of the post-production list, including the saved recordings and rendered results of other songs. Pending recordings, segment takes, song references and separated stems are not affected. Deleting cannot be undone; if the data matters, back up the whole recordings folder first.

  1. Besides the result, new recordings also save the clean vocals, playback timing, pitch observations and the reference / masks / scoring settings of the round. While recording they are kept in the browser; after stopping they are saved in the song library's recordings folder, for example D:\SIGN2MV\library\recordings. Each recording has its own folder with metadata.json, the mix audio and, when saved, the voice audio; after MP3 conversion export.mp3 is saved too. “Do not save recordings” keeps none of this.
  2. Go to 02 Recording post-production on the main screen and choose a saved recording; or press “Post-production” in the recordings list.
  3. Old recordings do not switch references automatically. Rescoring uses the reference from recording time by default. To compare with RMVPE: first load the RMVPE version of the same video and analysis range from the song library; new songs always build a whole-song RMVPE version; if the original recording is a clip, use a saved version with the same analysis range, since a whole-song version cannot replace it. Wait until “Ready” and RMVPE are shown, choose the original recording again in post-production, and set Reference for rescoring to Currently loaded reference. Check the version shown below, then rescore, without singing again. The song library version must finish loading; another video or a different analysis range cannot be applied. The result shows the pitch method actually used and the number of scorable melody seconds; the 0 ms comparison uses the same reference. Rescoring saves a snapshot of the reference used, and the recording's original reference, masks, scoring settings and mix backing source are kept. Switch back to “Reference saved with recording” to compare again.
  4. Choose the Rescoring difficulty: strict, standard or relaxed. It defaults to standard each time a recording is selected and is set in “Scoring settings”. The same recording can be rescored at different difficulties without singing again, without rewriting the original scoring settings or audio.
  5. Enter the Vocal timing correction: +100 ms moves the singer's voice 0.1 seconds earlier, −100 ms moves it later. Backing and harmonies do not move. The value is the total correction, calculated from the original data each time and never added up; it starts from the round's recording correction; old recordings use the timing compensation saved at the time.
  6. Press Adjust timing and rescore: the pitch is extracted again directly from the saved clean vocal audio and compared with the chosen reference on the same playback timeline used for remixing; the difficulty is the “Rescoring difficulty” choice; octave, scoring range and masks follow that round's settings. The result is labelled with and saves the difficulty used, and the 0 ms comparison uses the same difficulty. The new result is saved with this recording and the original score history is kept. The first rescore analyses the raw vocals in the background in the browser; later timing changes reuse that analysis, with no need to sing again. The page also shows the rhythm score of the same audio at 0 ms for comparison; the old live pitch timestamps are no longer used to judge a recording's rhythm. Live scoring and rescoring both compare the pitch observation nearest to each reference time, avoiding bias from sampling at the end of a step; after an update, existing post-production recordings can be rescored directly, without singing again or redoing the song reference.
  7. Press Render again (levels / timing / tone / trim): the clean vocals are moved to the new position and mixed with the instrumental and separate harmonies according to the original recording mode. Backing and harmonies share one volume; the manual / automatic balance from recording is kept and the volume is recalculated offline. Paused parts are not kept, and seeks are assembled in the order actually sung. Moving earlier trims vocals that fall before the start; moving later keeps the end.
  8. Afterwards a new WAV recording is saved and the original is not overwritten. Press play to preview it, then press Convert to MP3 and download. To keep adjusting, select this result directly; its volume, timing and smoothing settings are pre-filled and every render starts again from the kept raw vocals, so effects never stack. If the rescored rhythm clearly contradicts what you hear, “Download scoring diagnostics” exports a single JSON with the original recording, clean vocals, timing and reference. The file is only downloaded to this computer; with it the actual alignment can be checked without singing again. The uncorrected original recording can also be converted to MP3 directly.

MP3s are made by SIGN2MV's FFmpeg at 192 kbps, processed only on this computer and not uploaded. Temporary conversion files are removed after success or failure. Rendering with backing needs the song stems of the same version to be kept.

Old recordings or old results without separate vocals / timing data can still be downloaded and converted to MP3, but cannot be post-processed track by track. New-format results each save their raw vocals, timing data and production settings, so deleting the previous version does not stop them being edited again; the same-version backing in the song library must still be kept. A WAV / MP3 exported on its own does not contain this editing data; keep the full folder in the song library's recordings. If a new recording was not saved properly, post-production is disabled. Clearing a recording also deletes its clean vocals and scoring data; rendered versions saved as separate entries stay. Pitch detection, separation quality and browser timing errors still affect the results.

Trim, fades and advanced reverb

There are 12 space types: Legacy, Room, Hall, Plate, Studio, Chamber, Church, Stadium, Tunnel, Cave, Bathroom and Dream space. Each has its own synthetic reflections and tail, and a description of how it sounds is shown below the menu. Switching brings in a suggested decay and pre-delay, which can still be fine-tuned. Reverb level, brightness and width keep their current settings; reverb is heard only when its level is above 0%. The new spaces support A/B comparison, reset, and reopening after saving.

  1. Choose a recording in “Recording post-production”, press “Post-production” and open Trim and fades.
  2. Enter the start and end seconds to keep, or preview and press “Use current position”. The seconds refer to the full recording before trimming; for trimmed versions the player position is converted automatically. Leave the end empty to keep everything up to the end of the tail.
  3. Set the fade-in and fade-out seconds (0–30 each, together no longer than the kept length); they adjust vocals and backing together for a smooth start and end.
  4. In advanced vocal production, adjust the reverb level (0–100%; the higher the number, the more reverb is added, and the original vocals stay) and choose one of the 12 spaces. Switching space brings in suggested values first; decay can be set to 0.2–10 seconds and pre-delay to 0–500 ms. Pre-delay controls how long after the voice the reverb begins; it is not the timing correction that aligns the vocals with the backing.
  5. Press “Render again (levels / timing / tone / trim)” to save a copy, then preview it. The new file name shows the trim, fade and reverb settings; “Vocals only” also uses the saved effects and trim.

An explicit end time cuts the reverb after it. To let the tail end naturally, keep enough length or leave the end empty. Trimming does not change the raw vocals; press “Reset trim and fades” and render again to restore the full length. Vocal effects have their own reset button; the two work separately.

Each new-format result keeps its own raw vocals and settings, so repeated production never stacks effects, and it can still be edited after the previous version is deleted (mixes need the song library's backing kept). Rescoring still uses the full original performance; trimming does not change the scoring range. A WAV / MP3 downloaded on its own carries no restore data; keep the full recording folder in the song library's recordings.

Clip preview, A/B comparison and region effects

Vocal mood

In advanced vocal production, choose a “Vocal mood”: Original (default), or Tender and intimate, Sweet and joyful, Heartfelt, Nostalgic, Lonely, Sad and fragile, Calm and accepting, Confident and uplifting, Passionate, Dreamlike. A mood brings in EQ, smoothing and compression; the actual values are shown below the menu and can be fine-tuned, and once they are changed it shows “Custom tone”. These tones support the singing; they do not change how you sing or the pitch.

“Original” resets EQ to zero and turns smoothing and compression off; existing space effects such as reverb and delay stay, so it does not mean all production is off. Vocal mood and space preset are chosen separately, for example Heartfelt + Hall. New recordings start with Original in both sets; results with saved production bring back their saved tone. A and B keep their own, and the difference table lists the mood and its values. Press preview to compare and render again to save a copy; the new result's file name adds the mood name (omitted for Original); old recordings are not renamed or recalculated.

“Reset A to defaults” or “Reset B to defaults” (the button follows the set being edited) returns the current set to the system defaults: Original (EQ at zero, smoothing and compression off), reverb 0% (space type Hall, decay 1.8 s, pre-delay 25 ms, brightness 0, width 100%), delay 0% (300 ms / 3 repeats / 40% kept each time / centre), volume 100%, regions cleared, vocal cleanup and pitch correction off, and the timing correction back to the correction applied when this recording was made (the input latency). The other set, the shared trim and the preview range are kept, and saved recordings do not change. After a copy is rendered, A and B keep their own settings; only choosing another recording brings that recording's saved settings into both sets again.

Below the preview buttons, “A/B setting differences” is shown live: A in blue and B in purple, with the differing values side by side; identical items are hidden, and when the two sets are the same it says “A and B currently have the same settings”. Regions are compared one by one too, and a region one set does not have is marked “Not set”. The table reflects the current adjustments and does not change the sound already playing; after a change, press preview again. Trim and fades are shared and are not listed.

  1. Select a recording that can be post-processed and open “Clip preview / A/B comparison”. Enter the start and end of the same line in seconds of the full recording before trimming, up to 30 seconds per clip, and choose the mix or vocals only; or play the result first and press “Use 10 seconds from current position”.
  2. A (blue) and B (purple) start with exactly the same settings and are then adjusted separately for comparison; B is edited by default. Clicking an A / B tab or the switch buttons of the editing area recolours all volume, timing and effect fields and brings in that set's values; each set keeps its own adjustments. “Preview current settings on a clip” plays the set being edited. If a field is incomplete, the screen stays as it is; fix it, then switch.
  3. Switch with the large side-by-side A / B buttons, A in blue and B in purple; the set playing is highlighted and marked as the one you are hearing. The settings below use A / B tabs, each opening only its own set, and the left and right arrow keys switch tabs. The first time, the audio has to be prepared; after that the same position is kept and the clip loops. Approximate loudness matching lowers the louder set for the comparison only; it does not change the result.
  4. For the set you prefer, press “Go to A production settings” or “Go to B production settings” in its tab (or press “A settings” / “B settings” above the production area) to switch to that set, then press “Render again (levels / timing / tone / trim)” to save a copy. A/B previews are not written to the recordings list; switching recordings or reloading clears the comparison cache. Previews do not apply trim and fades yet; the result is still rendered with the trim settings.

Reverb brightness: negative values are softer and darker, positive values brighter, 0 follows the space default. Reverb stereo width: 0% is centred, 100% keeps the full left-right difference. Both change only the reverb, not the original vocals.

Delay: adjustable level, 80–1000 ms repeat interval, 1–8 repeats, the volume kept each time and left-right alternation. Keeping 40% each time means each repeat is four tenths of the one before; 0% leaves only the first. Used together with reverb, the two effects are generated from the vocals separately.

Region effects: press Add effect region and set start, end, reverb level, decay length and delay level. You can make verses lighter and choruses richer, or lengthen the tail only on the last line; up to 20 regions, no overlaps. Outside the regions the settings above apply, and tails already generated carry on naturally past a region. Brightness, width, space and delay interval still follow the global values.

All the new effects can be undone with “Reset all vocal effects” and rendered again from the raw vocals; saved original recordings are kept.

Backing gets quieter when the mic is on: native capture

  1. Open “Audio input” near the top of the page and set the input mode to “Native capture”. Choose your microphone.
  2. Press “Enable microphone”. If Windows asks for Python's microphone permission the first time, allow it.
  3. Play the video to check that the volume is normal and the input level reacts, then press “Sing from the start and score”. Scoring and recording work the same way.
  4. Stopping input or closing the tab stops native capture. When the window is covered, minimised or another tab is in front, a round that is starting, being sung or being scored keeps capturing; a microphone that is not in a round is released after 1 minute in the background. If the tool stops, an error is shown; enable it again.

This mode avoids the path where the browser opens the microphone hardware directly. The sound passes only between SIGN2MV's Python and the SIGN2MV window and is not uploaded; without saving recordings, no audio files are written. The mode choice is remembered, but capture still starts only when you press the button. This mode adds stream buffering, so recheck the timing compensation with “04 Latency calibration” or post-production instead of reusing old measurements. Only one tab at a time can use native capture.

Troubleshooting, step by step

Sing from the start and score does nothing / no sound is picked up

Check the microphone status first. Allow the permission when the SIGN2MV window asks for the microphone, then check the microphone privacy setting, input device and volume in Windows Settings. With native capture, also allow Python to use the microphone. Back on the page, press Enable microphone, and sing once the level reacts. If it is waiting for the player to sync, check that the video has loaded and shows no error.

Separated, but there are no preview stems

An old saved result may contain only the numeric reference. Open the separation preview, press Rebuild preview stems and wait for Ready; or keep the stems option ticked and separate again and overwrite. The lead and harmony buttons appear only for songs loaded in lead mode.

How do I preview old lead / harmony stems?

Load the saved lead version from the song library, then open “Separation and pitch settings · Masks and previews”. The lead and harmony buttons follow that version; if stems are missing, rebuild them first. New songs always use vocals / instrumental separation.

The page did not scroll to the player after Sing from the start and score

Scrolling happens after the microphone and player sync successfully. If it is still waiting for microphone permission, the permission was refused, or the player failed to sync, read the hints in the input and scoring blocks. Allow the microphone, check that the player can play, then press “Sing from the start and score” again.

I sang only a short part, but the score is very low

The default is now “Performed range only”. If you switched to “Whole song”, switch back before starting; whole-song mode counts the unsung rest as missed, and the range cannot be changed after a round starts. Also preview the reference and check the microphone and latency; the total score alone cannot tell you the cause.

Separation takes a long time and the percentage does not move

The first time, a model may be downloading; processing a whole song on the CPU also takes longer. Check the current stage and the GPU / CPU label. Stages without a real percentage do not show invented progress. The whole song is analysed by default; you can choose a short part in the advanced settings to compare first. To stop, press Cancel / Unload. Progress also shows in the SIGN2MV job queue.

Harmonies treated as lead / lead cut off

The lead / harmony model may have misjudged. Switch between previewing lead and vocals to see which matches the song better; you can go back to the default vocals / instrumental mode to build or load a reference. This tool is for practice and cannot guarantee accurate separation for every song.

When a lead-mode reference is built, harmonies that leaked into the lead stem are excluded automatically: within a continuous melody, if the harmony stem is clearly louder than the lead stem (a median difference of 6 dB or more), or the lead stem is almost silent (below −45 dBFS), that stretch is treated as a rest and not scored. Lead versions built before 2026-11-09 are rebuilt once automatically the next time they load (pitch only, about 1 minute). If lead singing is taken for harmonies and becomes a rest where you should sing, report the song title and the seconds.

Where is the data kept?

  • SIGN2MV song library (by default library in the SIGN2MV folder; the location can be changed in SIGN2MV “Settings”): songs, analysis ranges, separation models and modes, numeric references, unscored region masks and preview stems; Sing & Record recordings are in library\recordings, one folder each.
  • models in the SIGN2MV folder: the separation and RMVPE pitch models, downloaded at installation and shared by all songs.
  • The SIGN2MV window's browser data: score history, segment drafts, settings, and temporary data of recordings not yet saved.

All audio processing happens on this computer; nothing is sent to the cloud.

The “Vocals only” preview of whole-song and segment recordings plays the selected take's raw vocal file directly, without rendering or adjusting the balance or timing; only “Vocals + backing / harmonies” applies the mix balance and vocal correction (likewise for “Vocals + backing (no harmonies)” in a full mix). Add the original vocals to a mix

In “Choose takes and assemble → Assemble full mix → Original vocal regions”, enter a start and end time (min:sec) and press “Add original vocal region”. You can create several regions, each set to “Original + my recording” or “Original only”, and edit or delete them. Regions may not overlap; “Use current segment times” fills in the start and end of the selected segment.

They apply only to “Vocals + backing / harmonies” full mixes; outside the regions your own recording is used, and the instrumental keeps playing. The original vocals come from the separated vocal stem of this song's current key; vocal correction moves only your recording, and the original vocals stay on song time. With lead / harmony separation, the lead is added and the harmonies stay on the backing path.

Regions are saved with the segment draft of this song and key version. After a change, assemble again; downloading the WAV, saving for post-production and remixing keep the regions. The original vocals are never mixed into the raw microphone recordings, and vocals-only mixes do not apply the regions. The separated stems must be saved; “Original only” regions need no recording of your own.

Use a local video or audio file

In “Media source” above the player choose “Local video”, then choose an MP4, MOV, MKV or WebM file. The video must have sound and can be up to 2 GB and 15 minutes; incompatible formats are converted to a playable MP4 automatically. Importing copies it only into this computer's song library; the original file is kept.

After importing, press “Prepare song reference” to use scoring, singing from the start, segment recording, mixing and key changes. Next time just choose the song from the song library; videos without a reference yet can be loaded from “Imported videos”.

You can also choose “Local audio (MP3 / WAV)” and import an audio file from 1 second to 15 minutes long and up to 2 GB. The player shows the title and a default waveform picture, with play, pause and progress controls; singing and recording work as with a local video. The audio gets a 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV working copy; the original file is unchanged and nothing is uploaded to the cloud.

Working copies of videos and audio are in the song library's .local-mv folder. Deleting a song reference does not delete the working copy or the original file.
